# How Many Plants Does My Tank Need?

**Quick answer:** For a lightly planted look, plan on about **one plant for every 2 gallons** (a 20 gallon tank needs roughly 10). For a full, lush tank, plan on **one plant every 3 inches across the bottom** (roughly 25 to 40 in a 20 gallon long). Most people land in the middle. Start with more than you think, because a heavily planted tank is easier to keep free of algae than a sparse one.

How we worked these out:
- **Light planting** is about one plant per 2 gallons, with at least 4. It's in line with common beginner shopping lists, which suggest around 6 plants for a 10 gallon and 10 for a 20 gallon.
- **Full / jungle** is one plant every 3 inches across the bottom (length × depth ÷ 9). This is the spacing many experienced keepers use for a properly planted tank.
- **Medium** is halfway between the two.

Tank floor area matters more than gallons. A 20 long and a 29 have the same footprint, so they take about the same number of plants. A 20 high holds the same water as a 20 long but has less floor.

## Why more plants is usually better

- **Algae control.** Plants and algae compete for the same light and nutrients. A tank with a few plants leaves plenty of both for algae. A tank that's packed with fast growers takes most of it.
- **Cleaner water.** Growing plants take up nitrate and ammonia, the waste that builds up between water changes.
- **Easier recovery.** A few leaves melting or dying back after planting is normal. In a full tank, the rest carry on and you barely notice. In a sparse tank, one plant struggling is a third of your scape.
- **Shelter.** Fish and shrimp hide, rest and spawn in dense planting. Shy fish come out more when they have cover nearby.

## Split them by zone: foreground, midground, background

A simple rule that works for any tank size is roughly **20% foreground, 30% midground, 50% background**. Background plants are tall and fast, and they do most of the nutrient work.

| Zone | What it's for | Easy, no-CO2 picks we grow |
|---|---|---|
| **Background** (back third) | Height, fills the back glass, fastest growers | Water Wisteria (*Hygrophila difformis*), *Ludwigia repens*, *Bacopa caroliniana*, *Rotala indica*, Amazon sword |
| **Midground** (middle) | Bulk and texture | *Cryptocoryne wendtii* (green and bronze), narrow chain sword |
| **Foreground** (front) | Low cover, frames the tank | *Sagittaria subulata*, micro sword |
| **On wood or rock** (anywhere) | Low-light, slow growing, never buried | *Anubias nana*, Java fern |
| **Surface** (optional) | Shade, cover for bettas and fry, soaks up nitrate fast | Red root floaters, salvinia |

## Stem plants vs rooted plants: get a mix

- **Stem plants** (wisteria, ludwigia, bacopa, rotala) grow fast, fill space quickly and are cheap to buy in bundles. You trim them and replant the tops, so a few bundles become a lot of plants over time.
- **Rooted plants** (crypts, swords, sagittaria) grow slower but are long-lived and stay put. They feed from the gravel, so a root tab near them helps.
- **Attached plants** (anubias, java fern) are the toughest of all and grow in very low light.

A good starter tank is roughly **three quarters rooted and attached plants, one quarter stem plants**. The rooted plants give it structure and the stem plants do the fast nutrient work while the tank settles in. That's the mix we use in our tank-size bundles.

## Do I need special lights, CO2 or soil?

No. Every plant in this guide grows in:
- **Low to medium light** (the light that came with most aquarium kits is often enough for anubias, java fern and crypts; brighter is better for stem plants)
- **No added CO2**
- **Plain gravel or sand**, with root tabs pushed in near rooted plants
- **72 to 82 °F**

If you want faster growth later, a better light and a weekly all-in-one liquid fertilizer are the next upgrades. CO2 is optional.

## FAQ

**Can I have too many plants?**
Not really for fish. Plants make oxygen in the light and use a little at night, but even a jungle tank isn't a problem with normal surface movement from a filter. The practical limit is swimming room and how much trimming you want to do.

**Should I add all the plants at once?**
Yes, if you can. Planting heavily from day one gives algae the least chance to get started. If you're adding in stages, put the fast stem plants in first.

**How far apart should I plant stems?**
About 1 to 2 inches apart, each stem pushed an inch into the gravel. Don't plant a banded bunch as one clump; separate the stems first. (See [what to do when your plants arrive](/guides/aquarium-plants-arrival-guide).)

**Do plants count toward my fish stocking?**
Plants don't replace water changes or filtration, but a heavily planted tank handles waste better than a bare one.

**What if my tank isn't on the chart?**
Use the calculator above, or count floor area: length × depth in inches, divided by 9, is the plant count for a full tank.
