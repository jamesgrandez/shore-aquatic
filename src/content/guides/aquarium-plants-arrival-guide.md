# Your Plants Arrived: What to Do in the First 24 Hours

**Quick answer:** Open the box the day it arrives. Take a quick photo of the plants as they came out of the bag. Cut the bands off, rinse each plant, strip the bottom inch of leaves from stem plants and push them an inch into the gravel, 1 to 2 inches apart. Tie anubias and java fern to wood or rock instead of burying them. If anything arrived mushy or broken apart, send us the photo **within 24 hours of delivery** and we'll send a replacement or store credit.

## Before you start: 2-minute checklist

- [ ] Box opened the same day it was delivered
- [ ] Phone ready for a photo
- [ ] Scissors
- [ ] A bowl or bucket of **dechlorinated** water or tank water
- [ ] Tweezers (helpful, not required)
- [ ] Thread, fishing line or plant glue for anubias and java fern
- [ ] Root tabs, if you have them, for swords and crypts

## Step 1: Open the box right away

Live plants are packed damp, and they soften the longer they stay sealed in the dark. Even if you can't plant them right away, open the box, open the bags, and float the plants in a container of dechlorinated water near a light.

## Step 2: Take a photo as you unpack

One quick photo of the plants as they came out of the bag protects you if anything is wrong. If you need to report damage, that photo is what we ask for.

## Step 3: Cut every band and separate the stems

Bundles are held with a band or foam strip. **Cut it off every bundle** and separate the stems. A banded bundle planted as one clump holds water in the middle and rots from the inside.

![Shore Aquatic bundles separated into individual stems](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0266/9799/0330/files/71S6OBAZ85L._AC_SL1400.jpg?v=1794501622)
*Each bundle is several stems. Separated, they go a lot further.*

## Step 4: Rinse

Swish each plant in your bowl of dechlorinated water. Nursery-grown plants can carry a little duckweed or a tiny snail, so look them over as you rinse. If a pot or rock wool came with any plant, remove all of the rock wool.

## Step 5: Trim anything soft

A soft or mushy stem end is **not** a dead plant. Trim back to firm green tissue and plant the healthy top. Stem plants root from the cut. Pull off any leaves that are clearly mushy or translucent.

## Step 6: Plant by type

**Stem plants** (water wisteria, ludwigia, bacopa, rotala, anacharis)
1. Strip the leaves from the bottom inch of each stem.
2. Push that inch into the gravel or sand.
3. Space stems 1 to 2 inches apart so light reaches the lower leaves.

**Rosette and rooted plants** (crypts, swords, sagittaria)
1. Plant so the roots are covered but the crown (where the leaves meet the roots) sits at the gravel surface.
2. Push a root tab in nearby if you have one.

**Attached plants** (anubias, java fern)
1. **Don't bury the thick horizontal stem (rhizome).** Buried, it rots.
2. Tie it to driftwood or rock with thread or fishing line, or use a dab of gel superglue. Roots will grab on within a few weeks.

![An aquascaper planting anubias with tweezers](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/An_Aquascapist_Planting_Aquatic_Plant_Anubias_Petite_with_Tweezers_in_an_Aquarium.jpg?width=1200)
*Anubias goes on wood or rock, not in the gravel.*

**Floating plants** (red root floaters, salvinia)
Just set them on the surface. Keep them away from strong filter flow so they don't get pushed under.

## Step 7: Lights and the first two weeks

- Keep your light on a normal schedule, about **6 to 8 hours a day**. More light won't help plants recover faster and can feed algae.
- Don't add new fertilizer for the first few days.
- Do your normal water changes.

## What's normal and what's damage

Shipping is a big change for a plant. Here's how to tell the difference, using the same terms as our [live arrival guarantee](/shipping-returns).

| Normal transit stress (no action needed) | Damage (send us a photo) |
|---|---|
| A few slightly yellowed leaves | Mushy plant |
| A few bruised or bent stems | Fully melted plant |
| A tired, limp look on arrival | Plant broken apart |
| Older leaves dropping over the first 1 to 3 weeks | |

**Melting is common and temporary.** Many plants are grown with their leaves out of the water, and they drop some old leaves while they grow new underwater ones. Trim damaged leaves, leave the rhizome or crown in place, and new growth usually shows within 2 to 3 weeks. More in [Why are my aquarium plants melting?](/blog/aquarium-plants-melting) and [Why are my plants turning brown, yellow or black?](/blog/aquarium-plants-turning-brown-yellow-black)

## FAQ

**I can't plant them today. What should I do?**
Open the bags, float the plants in a container of dechlorinated water, and keep them near a light. Most will hold for a day or two like that. Don't leave them sealed in the box.

**Should I dip or treat new plants?**
A thorough rinse and a look-over is enough for most tanks. If you keep shrimp or want to be extra careful, hold the plants in a separate container for a week or two before adding them.

**Why are my stem plants floating up?**
They weren't pushed deep enough. Strip a bit more of the lower stem and push it in further, or plant two stems together in one hole for grip until roots form.

**My crypts lost all their leaves. Are they dead?**
Usually not. Crypts are known for melting after a move. Leave the roots in the gravel and new leaves usually come back from the base.

**How long until plants start growing?**
Fast stem plants often show new tips within a week or two. Rooted plants and anubias take longer.
