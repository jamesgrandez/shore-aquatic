# How We Ship Live Plants in Winter and Summer

**Quick answer:** Yes, you can order live aquarium plants in winter. We pack every order in an **insulated box**, add a **heat pack** in cold weather or a **cool pack** in hot weather based on the forecast where you live, and ship **Monday to Wednesday** only, so plants don't sit in a warehouse over the weekend. Your part is simple: get the box inside soon after it's delivered, and open it the same day.

## How we pack every order

1. **Wrapped damp.** Plants are wrapped in moisture-holding material so they stay wet without sitting in water.
2. **Sealed.** Each wrap goes into a sealed poly bag.
3. **Insulated box.** Bags go into an insulated box, which slows down temperature changes in both directions.
4. **Heat or cool pack when needed.** We check the forecast for your area when we pack. Cold forecast: a heat pack goes in. Hot forecast: a cool pack goes in.

## Why temperature matters

Most aquarium plants are tropical. They're happiest between about 72 and 82 °F in your tank, and they handle a cool, dark box for a couple of days much better than heat. The risks are at the extremes:

- **Freezing** damages leaves and stems. Ice crystals burst the cells and the plant turns to mush.
- **Heat** cooks plants in a sealed bag and speeds up rotting. A box sitting in the sun on a porch can get much hotter than the air.

That's why the whole hobby uses the same basic tools: insulation, heat packs in cold weather, cool packs in hot weather, and short transit times.

## Why we don't add a heat pack every time

A heat pack in mild weather can do more harm than good. Inside an insulated box with nothing cold to offset it, it can overheat the plants. Shippers across the hobby generally add heat packs only once temperatures get down to around 40 °F, and cool packs once they climb to around 90 °F. That's why we decide per order, based on your forecast, instead of putting one in every box.

| Weather at your door | What usually goes in the box |
|---|---|
| Cold (around 40 °F and below) | Heat pack |
| Mild | Insulated box only |
| Hot (around 90 °F and above) | Cool pack |

## What you can do to help

- **Track your package** and plan to bring it inside within a few hours of delivery. This matters most in very cold or very hot weather.
- **Ship to where someone will be.** A workplace or a neighbor can beat a porch in January or July.
- **Watch for big cold snaps.** In extreme cold it can make sense to wait a few days for a warmer stretch before you order.
- **Open the box the same day.** Then follow our [first 24 hours guide](/guides/aquarium-plants-arrival-guide).

## If something goes wrong

Every order is covered by our live arrival guarantee. If plants arrive damaged (mushy, fully melted or broken apart), send a photo **within 24 hours of delivery** and we'll send a replacement or store credit. A few slightly yellowed leaves or a tired look after the trip is normal and usually recovers in a couple of weeks. Full details are on our [Shipping & Returns page](/shipping-returns).

## FAQ

**Is winter a bad time to buy aquarium plants?**
No. With insulation, a heat pack and short transit, plants ship well in winter. Cooler weather is often easier on plants than summer heat.

**Can I ask for a heat pack?**
We add heat packs ourselves based on your local forecast when we pack the order. If you have a specific concern, such as a package sitting outside all day, [contact us](/contact) before we ship.

**What if my package is delayed?**
Plants are packed to handle normal 1 to 2 day transit. Open the box as soon as it arrives and check the plants. If anything is damaged, send us a photo within 24 hours of delivery.

**The heat pack was cold when I opened the box. Is that a problem?**
Heat packs run for a set time and are designed to cover the trip. A cool pack being warm or a heat pack being cool on arrival is normal after transit. What matters is the condition of the plants.

**Do you ship fish or livestock the same way?**
This guide covers plants. Livestock has its own shipping requirements. Check the product page or contact us.
